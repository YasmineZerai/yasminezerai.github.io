## Problem

The team reviewed sensor data from every hive by hand to find colonies in trouble. A low health score didn't always mean a sick colony, and a false alarm costs a beekeeper a real drive.

## What I built

An **async FastAPI** backend on **MongoDB** (**106 REST endpoints**, **94 automated tests**), a **React** dashboard in English and Arabic, and three parts that carry it:

- **The MQTT service** that listens to every device, updates its live state and fires immediate alerts, and sends commands back to the hives.
- **An autonomous ReAct agent** that investigates anomalies every night with 7 tools. It **replaced the manual review of sensor data completely**.
- **The colony health score**, 0 to 10, computed every night for **early detection of colony collapse**.

## The decisions that matter

<Decision title="Rules first, so cost follows anomalies, not fleet size">
A deterministic rule engine checks every device for free, with bilingual template alerts. Only brood anomalies escalate to the agent, and a healthy fleet makes **no LLM call at all**.
</Decision>

<Decision title="The agent can fail. The alert can't.">
The answer must parse against a schema; when it doesn't, the error goes back to the model and it **corrects itself**. If it still fails, the template alert goes out instead. Every tool call is stored, so **any alert can be traced back to its evidence**.
</Decision>

<Decision title="One MQTT listener, outside the API">
The listener runs as its own service, so there's **exactly one subscriber** however many API replicas run, and each alert is handled once. Its callback never does I/O, so a slow email can't block the connection to the devices.
</Decision>

### My contribution to the health score: Brood Presence Confidence

I found the problem in the data, not in the formula. Exploring the fleet's **historical sensor recordings**, I saw **a large number of hives scoring near zero without being unhealthy**: they had no brood, or the probe had moved, and the score read "no brood signal" as "sick colony".

So I treated it as a data science problem. I did the **EDA** and **feature engineering** on the sensor time series, ran **correlation analysis** between brood, hive and outside temperatures, and used a **gradient-boosted regression** to measure how much outside weather explains what the hive sensors read. That gave three behaviours bees show when they raise brood, which became a confidence **c ∈ [0, 1]** that the probe is really reading brood, **calibrated against the fleet's history**. When c is low, brood penalties drop, so **a displaced probe or a natural brood break produces uncertainty instead of a false critical alert**. It's **now in production**.

### The MQTT service

<Diagram id="beekeeper-mqtt" />

- **Old data never overwrites new.** Retained messages are skipped on reconnect so old alerts don't re-fire, a timestamp guard keeps the freshest reading, and a persistent session lets the broker queue messages while the listener restarts.
- **Commands wait for a sleeping hive.** Commands go out with at-least-once delivery and the retain flag, so a device that wakes up still gets the last one.
- MQTT is the fast path. A nightly job owns the history: it ingests device logs, preprocesses them, scores every hive and writes the reports.

### The platform

- **Fleet overview:** health distribution, a map of every hive, the 5 best and 5 worst colonies.
- **Hive detail:** time-series charts, the score gauge with its sub-scores, anomaly flags, an AI-written summary with prioritised actions, PDF export.
- **Three roles** (beekeeper, organisation, administrator), with scope enforced by the backend.
- **Notifications** in-app, on WhatsApp and by email.
- **Field work:** QR codes on hives to log inspections, 8 inspection types, bulk operations.
- **Fully bilingual English and Arabic, right-to-left included**, down to alerts, AI analysis and PDF reports from a **RAG** pipeline (LangChain, embeddings, semantic chunking).
